# Data plan — synthetic corpus for the Hebrew-in-Latin token classifier

Written 2026-09-22. Supersedes reliance on the vendored source dataset as the primary
corpus; see "Why generate rather than repair" below.

## Goal

A span-annotated corpus of English sentences containing Hebrew words written in Latin
characters, large enough and clean enough to fine-tune `xlm-roberta-base` for token
classification, and published on Hugging Face as a reusable artefact.

It should be the corpus that does not currently exist anywhere — no Hebrew-English
code-switching corpus is published, token-tagged or otherwise.

## Why generate rather than repair

The vendored dataset (`danielrosehill/English-Hebrew-Mixed-Sentences`) has 516 records
of which 360 carry a usable label; the other 156 need a human, and its 139-term
vocabulary is small and partly contaminated. Repairing it is ~242 review tasks and
yields at most 516 sentences over 139 terms.

Generating gives more terms, controlled category balance, explicit hard negatives, and
spans that are known by construction rather than recovered by matching — for a
comparable amount of human review. The existing dataset is not discarded: its 360
aligned records join the corpus as a human-read, independently-sourced slice, and its
audio makes it the only part usable for future speech work.

## Target size

| Slice | Sentences | Terms | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Positives — one Hebrew term | 1,500 | ~500 distinct | 3 sentences per term |
| Positives — two or more terms | 300 | reuses the same pool | The source dataset's main gap; real speech code-switches more than once per sentence |
| Hard negatives | 500 | — | See below. The expensive class to get right |
| Plain negatives | 300 | — | Ordinary English, no Hebrew. Keeps the base rate honest |
| **Generated total** | **2,600** | | |
| Vendored, aligned records | 360 | 117 | Human-read, independent provenance |
| **Corpus total** | **~2,960** | **~500** | |

**Why ~2,600 and not 500 or 20,000.** The EnTaCs English-Tamil recipe (arXiv
2603.26587) is the closest published comparison: ~500 utterances, ~3,500 tokens,
three labels, `xlm-roberta-base` — macro-F1 0.844 with the romanized class at
**F1 0.638**. That 0.638 is the number to beat, and roughly 5× the data is the
cheapest lever available. Beyond a few thousand sentences, returns on synthetic data
of a single generator's style fall off faster than returns on term coverage, so the
budget goes into **more terms**, not more sentences per term.

**Term coverage matters more than sentence count.** FR-4 wants the classifier to
generalise to terms it has not seen. The splits are therefore stratified **by term,
not by sentence**: no term appears in both train and test. Splitting by sentence would
measure memorisation and report a number 20-30 points too high.

| Split | Share | Terms |
| --- | --- | --- |
| train | 80% | disjoint |
| validation | 10% | disjoint |
| test | 10% | disjoint |

## Term inventory

Target ~500 distinct terms, from:

1. The 117 active terms already in `data/lexicon.csv`.
2. Terms measured in the *My Weird Prompts* transcript corpus — *shekel*, *Knesset*,
   *haredi*, *mamad*, *shuk*, *arnona*, *aliyah*, *yeshiva*, *miluim*, *protektzia*.
3. Generated by category, to fill the registers the existing data is thin on.

Category balance is deliberate. The vendored dataset is skewed institutional and
religious — the register an English voice half-copes with. The register that actually
fails is colloquial, and it is nearly absent (in 4,295 MWP episodes: *makolet* 1
occurrence, *balagan* 1, *beseder* 0, *kol hakavod* 0).

## Hard negatives — the class that decides precision

FR-7 weights precision over recall: a miss changes nothing, a false positive makes an
English word be read in Hebrew. Negatives therefore get real budget.

| Kind | Count | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Anglicised Hebrew — must be negative | 150 | *kosher*, *Shabbat*, *hummus*, *rabbi*, *kibbutz*, *chutzpah*, *bagel* |
| English homographs of Hebrew terms | 150 | *at*, *hi*, *lo*, *ken*, *ma*, *mi*, *baby*, *chicken soup* used as ordinary English |
| Hebrew-looking English or other loanwords | 100 | *chalet*, *cholera*, *shaman*, *sheikh*, *bazaar*, *falafel* if ruled anglicised |
| Substring traps | 100 | Sentences containing *think*, *Pharm*, *moshav*, *mazgan*, *machsom*, *school* — the exact strings that corrupted the source labels |

The substring traps exist specifically so the classifier is trained against the failure
mode that produced the vendored dataset's 114 bad labels.

## Generation

**Route: OpenRouter**, `OPENROUTER_API_KEY` from the environment. Verified live
2026-09-22 (`is_free_tier: false`, real usage history, non-null `creator_user_id`).

**Batch inference is available and halves the cost.** OpenRouter exposes `:batch`
model variants — `anthropic/claude-sonnet-5:batch` is $1/M in, $5/M out against
$2/$10 for the interactive variant. Same for the OpenAI and Opus families. The
generator takes `--model`, so the batch variant is a flag, not a rewrite. Batch
requests trade latency for price; for a corpus build that is the right trade.

**Term-seeded, not free-form.** The generator is given a term and asked for sentences
using it, rather than asked to "write sentences with Hebrew words". This:

- guarantees term coverage and category balance instead of hoping for it;
- makes the span **known by construction** — the seed term is the answer, and a
  generation where the term does not word-boundary match is rejected outright;
- avoids the generator's own bias toward the handful of Hebrew words it knows best.

Structured output (`response_format: json_schema`) so parsing never guesses.

**Cost estimate.** ~2,600 sentences at ~150 output tokens each is ~400K output tokens
for generation, and similar for annotation. On `claude-sonnet-5:batch` at $5/M output
that is roughly **$4 for the whole corpus**, both passes. This is not a budget
constraint; do not optimise it at the cost of quality.

## Annotation — generate, annotate independently, adjudicate

The standard AI-annotation pattern, and the reason the human queue stays small:

```
[1] generate       seed term -> sentences                  (model A)
[2] verify         seed term word-boundary matches?        (deterministic, no model)
[3] annotate       label ALL Hebrew spans, blind to seed    (model B, different family)
[4] adjudicate     compare [2] and [3]
                     agree    -> auto-accepted
                     disagree -> human review queue
[5] audit          10% random sample of agreements -> queue
[6] apply          -> BIO corpus
```

**Model B must be a different family from model A**, and must not be told the seed
term. Two passes from the same model agreeing tells you the model is consistent, not
that it is right. Annotating blind is what makes agreement evidence.

Step 3 is what catches the source dataset's other defect: incidental Hebrew terms the
seeding did not intend. Model B labels every Hebrew span it sees, so a sentence seeded
with *arnona* that also happens to contain *iriyah* comes back with both, and the
disagreement with the single-term seed sends it to review.

**Step 5 is not optional.** Without a random audit of agreements there is no estimate
of how wrong the auto-accepted majority is — only a measure of how often two models
disagree, which is a different and much less useful number.

## Review

Reuses the existing UI — `review/index.html`, served by `scripts/serve_review.py`.
Keyboard-driven, progress in `localStorage`, export to `review/decisions.json`.

Expected volume:

| Queue | Estimate |
| --- | --- |
| Disagreements between seed and annotator | ~300-400 (12-15% of 2,600) |
| Random audit of agreements | 260 (10%) |
| **Total** | **~600 tasks** |

At the rate the original voice note assumed — a thousand annotations reviewed quickly
in a basic GUI — this is one sitting, and it is spent on the cases that are actually
ambiguous rather than on 2,600 obvious ones.

Disagreement rate is itself a diagnostic. Under 5% means the annotator is agreeing too
easily and the prompt or the model needs changing. Over 25% means the annotation policy
is underspecified — go and settle the UNDECIDED cases in `docs/annotation-policy.md`
rather than reviewing 600 tasks inconsistently.

## What this plan does not do

- **No audio.** Text task. The vendored WAVs are not copied and none are generated.
- **No Hebrew script.** The `hebrew_script` column in the lexicon stays empty here;
  populating it is stage 2 of the planning repo, not this one.
- **No model training.** This plan ends at a published corpus. Training is the next
  document.
